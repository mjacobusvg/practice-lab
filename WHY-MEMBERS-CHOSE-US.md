# WHY-MEMBERS-CHOSE-US.md — voice of customer

A running list of the real reasons prescribers chose (or are choosing) Think Beyond
Practice, in their own words. This is raw material for `MARKETING-SPINE.md`, the landing
page, onboarding, ads, and the reply variants. Quotes here are evidence; the sharpest,
clearest ones graduate into the marketing spine.

House style when you reuse these: **no em-dashes.** Keep quotes verbatim in this file; do
not polish a member's words in the list itself. Polish only when you graduate a line into
the pitch.

## How to use

- Add an entry whenever a member or prospect tells you why: welcome-email replies, DMs,
  forum posts, calls, demo feedback. Downgrade and cancel notes count too (they tell you
  what did NOT land).
- Keep the quote verbatim. Record who said it, their tier/status, the date, and where it
  came from.
- Tag the theme(s) so patterns surface (see Themes tally).
- When a theme keeps recurring, pull the sharpest quote into `MARKETING-SPINE.md` and note
  it here under "Graduated to the pitch."
- Get permission before using a real name or quote publicly. Anonymize when unsure
  ("a PMHNP starting solo practice").

## Entries

### 2026-09-22 / 2026-09-24 — Victoria "Vicki" Hensley (prospect; new PMHNP; trial starts when she begins seeing patients, ~30 days out)

First reply (Sep 22):
> "This program this far looks like my highest contender."
> "I am excited to try this software as it seems to fill all the needs I have been searching for."

Full "why" (Sep 24), verbatim highlights:

- On the ethos (Scribe stops before the assessment; support reasoning, do not replace it):
  > "it feels like it was built to support the way a psych provider actually thinks and works, rather than just being another program that listens to a visit and spits out a note. The way you described the Scribe stopping before the assessment really clicked for me. I want help as I'm learning and building confidence, but I don't want AI doing the clinical thinking for me. I would much rather have it bring something to my attention, make me stop and think about it, let me make the decision, and then help me put that reasoning into a strong assessment."
  > "I actually think that is probably more valuable to me right now than having something simply generate a beautiful note."

- On continuity / pre-visit prep:
  > "Having the program look at the previous visit and remind me about things I wanted to follow up on is huge... a patient walking in with three new concerns and me realizing afterward that I never asked whether they actually tried the medication change we discussed last time. Having those open threads in front of me before and during the visit feels incredibly useful."

- On Therapy Coach / psychotherapy documentation:
  > "there are a lot of therapeutic things I naturally do in conversations with patients, but I may not always recognize exactly what technique I'm using or know how to appropriately translate that into psychotherapy documentation. I love the idea of the program helping me become more intentional with those skills rather than just creating a psychotherapy note after the fact."
  (Background she shared: first Master's in clinical psychology, then MN nursing; the therapeutic work is second nature, but phrasing it in "the words insurance is looking for" is effortful, so she often just would not bill for work she was already doing.)

- On Chart Audit + Coder as a learning tool:
  > "I really like being able to see whether my documentation actually supports what I'm billing, where my reasoning might be too thin, or what I could have documented better. That feels much more like a learning tool than just a coding calculator."

- On the whole workspace:
  > "having the screeners, medication interaction tools, safety planning, LAI guidance, and the other psych-specific resources available in the same place. The more I look at it, the more it feels like a psych workspace or copilot rather than simply a scribe, and that is really what I've been looking for."

- On peer support / mentoring:
  > "having access to a community of psychiatric providers and people I can learn from is incredibly appealing. I also genuinely hope to become a mentor myself one day."

- The line that sums it up:
  > "I don't just want something that makes me faster. I want something that helps me become better while still making my life easier, and that is really what keeps pulling me back to Think Beyond."

Theme(s): support-reasoning-not-replace-it (the audit/assessment ethos); continuity / pre-visit
prep; psychotherapy coaching + documentation of work already being done; coding confidence as
learning, not a calculator; all-in-one psych workspace / copilot (not just a scribe);
peer support + mentoring; affordability for a new, low-caseload PMHNP; "become better, not
just faster."

Source: reply to the free welcome-sequence email, after Michael asked what specifically was
drawing her and what she was comparing against.

## Competitive intel

- **OnDemand Psych** — Vicki's other top choice. She was impressed by its depth and clinical
  support, and it seemed "especially strong with more complex cases." Her blocker on it was
  **cost**: as a new PMHNP without the caseload yet, the monthly expense is hard to justify.
  TBP wins for her on price-for-where-she-is plus the growth/learning ethos.
  > "Think Beyond feels much more realistic for where I am right now and, more importantly, it feels like something that could actually help me grow into the kind of PMHNP I want to be."

## Product / funnel signals (from real prospects)

- **Trial-clock friction:** several features are locked until she activates the trial, and she
  is hesitant to start the clock before she is seeing real patients, because she wants to trial
  ambient quality and editing burden against real visits. Signal: consider a sandbox/"play
  around" mode (sample charts, no clock) so evaluators can explore before committing the trial.
  Michael is inclined to grant her early access on that basis.
- **Preflight depth:** her single strongest draw is the "stop and make me think before the
  assessment" flow. Note (Michael, Sep 24): the pre-visit preflight questions were dumbed down
  earlier because they felt like they slowed note generation, but her feedback suggests that
  richer, reasoning-eliciting preflight IS the ethos of the Scribe. Worth revisiting whether the
  fuller preflight should return (ideally optional / fast-path toggle) rather than stay trimmed.
