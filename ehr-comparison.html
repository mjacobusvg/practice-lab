How to use this: start by deciding whether you need a full medical EHR or a therapy-first platform with prescribing add-ons. If you prescribe and bill insurance, pay special attention to the eRx/EPCS, documentation, claim visibility, clearinghouse, and contract rows.

Figures are member-reported quotes from specific dates (noted per cell), not vendor list prices. EHR pricing changes often, varies by specialty and volume, and is negotiable. Verify with the vendor and get tiers and thresholds in writing before signing.
Watch the fine print on claim volume. Several EHRs price by claim volume or claim transactions, not by unique patient encounters. The difference matters. "One encounter = one claim" is industry standard. "Every claim transaction (including corrections, resubmissions, secondaries, rebills, and denials) = a separate count" is a different pricing structure that can drive costs up sharply for high-volume providers, providers working denials thoroughly, or medical-specialty providers generating multiple claims per encounter (labs, vaccines, in-office procedures, point-of-care testing). Before signing with any EHR that uses volume-tiered pricing, get the counting method and tier thresholds in writing.

Quick read

For solo or small practices wanting a full medical EHR with strong billing visibility: Tebra. For multi-provider groups, get claim-transaction definitions and volume thresholds in writing before signing, since per-transaction counting can scale costs sharply. If group pricing is the pain point and you want predictable per-provider cost with admin users included: AdvancedMD. If you want done-for-you billing and can replace existing billing labor in the comparison: Athena. If you want therapy-first simplicity and can live with the workflow ceiling for medical complexity: TherapyNotes or SimplePractice. If you need native prescribing, be careful with Jane. If cost is the priority and you can tolerate setup friction and weak support: Charm. And if a system already fits your brain, like DrChrono did for one of our members, familiarity is worth real money.
