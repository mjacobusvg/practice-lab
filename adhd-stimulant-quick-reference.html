ADHD Stimulant Quick Reference

Choosing the formulation for the problem in front of you.

Most of us prescribe from a small stimulant toolbox, and usually for good reasons. This card is for the moment you wonder whether one of the other formulations solves a problem yours does not. It is organized by the problem, not by the drug.

Last reviewed September 2026. Formulation details and labeling change — verify dosing and current indications against the label before prescribing.

Decision support, not a directive. This is a reference aid for licensed prescribers. It does not replace your clinical judgment, a full evaluation, or current prescribing information. Duration and onset figures describe what was measured in studies, not what any individual patient will experience. Coverage and availability vary by plan, state and pharmacy and are not addressed here.

1. What problem am I actually trying to solve?

A medication change should solve a named problem. If you cannot say what the next formulation should do differently, the change is a guess.

The line that sticks: "Failed Adderall" is where useful prescribing information goes to die. Name the failure and the next clinician inherits something worth having.

2. The familiar six

Familiar for good reasons: you know how they behave, how to titrate them, and what patients can usually get.

Adderall IR
Roughly four to six hours. Reach for it when precise timing, a variable schedule, or a short extension is the point.
Adderall XR
Two releases from one capsule; benefit measured through twelve hours. Many are covered all day, many fade by midafternoon. Both are normal.
Vyvanse
Prodrug converted to dextroamphetamine. Benefit measured from two hours through fourteen. Often described as gradual.
Methylphenidate IR
Roughly three to five hours. The clean way to answer whether methylphenidate helps at all before choosing a delivery system.
Concerta
Early portion then gradually increasing release; classic studies found roughly ten to twelve hours.
Focalin XR
Dexmethylphenidate, the active isomer, so milligrams look smaller. Label advises starting at half the current total methylphenidate dose.

The EHR trap: generic Mydayis and generic Adderall XR both appear as amphetamine/dextroamphetamine ER. The strengths give it away — Adderall XR is 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 mg; Mydayis is 12.5, 25, 37.5, 50 mg. A stray 37.5 mg is the Mydayis-type product, and the label says not to substitute one for the other milligram-for-milligram.

4. Before you call it a stimulant failure

  • Ask when, not just what. A side effect at the peak points to dose or release shape. One that follows a patient across both families points away from another stimulant altogether. One that appears only on wear-off is a different conversation entirely.
  • Rule out what else changed. Manufacturer, pharmacy, sleep, alcohol or cannabis, a harder job, an untreated mood or anxiety problem, another medication. Each can make a working stimulant look like a failing one, and none should automatically be answered with a higher dose.
  • Watch the interactions. Fluoxetine and paroxetine are strong CYP2D6 inhibitors and change amphetamine exposure. With atomoxetine the same pairing can raise exposure several-fold.
  • Do not titrate into a manufacturer change. Stable for months, new manufacturer, sudden change — find out what was dispensed first.
  • An intolerable medication that improved function still worked. Record it that way.
The study clock is not the patient's clock. "Benefit at twelve hours" means the treated group still differed from placebo at that measurement. It does not mean every patient gets twelve hours, and the patient who reliably gets seven good hours does not have an abnormal metabolism.

5. Access is part of the decision

There is nothing unsophisticated about staying with familiar generics when they work. Friction shapes prescribing, and the clever formulation only helps if the patient can actually get it.

  • Generic mixed amphetamine salts, lisdexamfetamine, methylphenidate and dexmethylphenidate generally have the smoothest operational path.
  • Newer branded delivery systems are where prior authorizations, step therapy and copays concentrate.
  • Generic approval and routine availability at the counter are not the same thing, and supply can change quickly.
  • Check current coverage and availability before building a plan around any less common product. Nothing on this card can tell you what a given plan will pay for.
