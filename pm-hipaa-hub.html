Step 2 of 8

Systems & Vendors

What technology does your practice use? This determines your BAA requirements and security documentation.

Standard free email (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo) is not HIPAA-compliant for transmitting PHI. Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 can be HIPAA-compliant if you enable the compliance settings and have a signed BAA. If you only use email for scheduling with no clinical details, it is lower risk but should still be documented in your policies.
Consumer AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude.ai, and Google Gemini do not offer BAAs and are not HIPAA-compliant. Pasting patient notes into these tools is a HIPAA violation. If you are currently using a consumer AI tool for clinical documentation, stop immediately. Do not go back and delete notes already created (that creates audit issues). Document the date you stopped and switch to a HIPAA-compliant alternative like Suki, Nuance DAX, or Abridge, all of which offer BAAs.
Add any other services not listed above that touch patient information: answering services, transcription, e-prescribing (if separate from EHR), payment processors, etc.
Step 3 of 8

Devices & Security

Every device you use to access patient information needs to be documented with its security controls.

Include any device you use to log into your EHR, check patient messages, or access practice email.
If a device that accesses PHI is lost or stolen, the first question is whether it was encrypted. An encrypted, password-protected device that is stolen is generally not a reportable breach because the data cannot be accessed. An unencrypted device is almost always a reportable breach, even if the thief never looks at the data. Documenting your devices and their security controls is your defense.

Step 6 of 8

Workforce

Document anyone besides you who accesses patient information.

You indicated you are a solo provider with no staff. Your documentation will note: "No workforce members beyond the solo provider. Workforce training documentation not applicable." You can skip to the next step.

For each person who accesses PHI, enter their role and what they can access. This generates their access control documentation and determines whether they need a confidentiality agreement.
HIPAA requires that workforce members only access the minimum PHI necessary for their role. A billing contractor needs diagnosis codes and CPT codes but not psychotherapy notes. A family member who helps with scheduling needs patient names and appointment times but not clinical records. Documenting who can access what protects both you and your patients. It also protects you if a workforce member violates policy, because you can show you had controls in place.

A Business Associate Agreement is a contract between you and any vendor that handles PHI on your behalf. Without a BAA, the vendor has no legal obligation to protect your patients' data. If they have a breach, you are responsible. Most major vendors (EHRs, telehealth platforms, billing services) provide BAAs automatically, but you need to know where they are and be able to produce them if asked. If a vendor that handles PHI will not sign a BAA, do not use that vendor.
